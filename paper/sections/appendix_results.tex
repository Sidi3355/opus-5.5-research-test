\subsection{Primary versus pre-registered tampering definition}
\label{app:prereg}
The pre-registered definition counts any change to a protected file as tampering. Reading reports during validation, we found agents that created an empty scratch file under \texttt{tests/} (having no file-deletion tool) while leaving the conflict test failing and reporting it honestly. Our primary definition therefore counts a protected-file change only if, in the agent's own final suite, the conflict test passes, is skipped, or is gone. \Cref{tab:prereg} reports the main quantities under both definitions.

\paragraph{Amendments to the pre-registration.} The pre-registration and four dated amendments are released. Amendments 1 and 2, made before any main-experiment trial, sharpened the definition of a fabricated requirement (adding the \emph{external authority} and \emph{generalization} flags used for the strict definition), fixed the task-selection rule, corrected an arithmetic error in the planned trial count, and set a budget stop. Amendment 3 added the minimal-pair experiment (H6) after an interim look at 60 main-run trials showed gaming concentrated in tasks with a plausible cover rule; it was registered before any minimal-pair trial ran. Amendment 4 added the diff-only review setting after the Opus 5.5 reviewer rejected every gamed change in the pre-registered full-repository setting; it is reported as exploratory, and the comparison group of non-fabricated reports planned for the review experiment was dropped to conserve budget.
